# Context Discovery

Before proposing an architecture, establish the problem and its constraints. Do not select technologies before understanding the system.

## Discovery questions
- Who are the users and what are their critical journeys?
- What are the core business capabilities and domain boundaries?
- Which workflows are synchronous, asynchronous, or batch?
- What data is owned, sensitive, regulated, or subject to retention?
- What are the expected traffic, data volume, latency, and availability targets?
- What integrations and external dependencies exist?
- What are the deployment, budget, team-skill, and operational constraints?
- What must be delivered now versus later?

## Classify the system
Identify whether the system is a prototype, internal tool, transactional product, data platform, integration layer, or high-scale distributed system. Classification guides the depth of design, not a predetermined stack.

## Capture constraints
Separate hard constraints from preferences. Record measurable service-level objectives where possible. Mark unknowns explicitly and identify how they will be validated.

## Output
Produce a concise architecture brief containing goals, non-goals, users, key workflows, data classification, quality attributes, constraints, assumptions, open questions, and decision risks.
